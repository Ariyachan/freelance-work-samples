ORIGINAL DOCUMENTARY WRITING SAMPLE — 11 October 2026
RMS Nova Scotia (1926)
A short original opening, not a completed commissioned script.

INTRO

On 28 November 1942, a German submarine sank a British ship off southern Africa. Then the Germans heard Italian voices in the water.

Two Italian merchant sailors were taken aboard U-177. They came from Germany's ally, but had been travelling on an enemy's ship. Most of the people who had sailed with them would never reach land.

The attack was over in minutes. Its consequences would reach far beyond the bridge of a British ship or the control room of a German submarine. Who were the people aboard Nova Scotia? Why were so many Italians on her last voyage? And who would come to help when the ship was gone?

CHAPTER 1 — BEFORE THE WAR

Nova Scotia entered service in 1926, carrying passengers and mail across the Atlantic. Her route linked Liverpool with St John's, Halifax and Boston. A timetable turned an ocean crossing into something people could plan their lives around.

War changed what those journeys meant. Requisitioned in 1941, the liner became a troopship. The same hull now moved people through a military transport system reaching far beyond the Atlantic.

By November 1942, her destination was Durban. Many aboard were being carried into internment. To understand their fate, we have to follow the ship away from her familiar Atlantic route, into another ocean where the war was closing in.
