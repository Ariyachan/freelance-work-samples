INDEPENDENT SCREENPLAY STUDY - 26 September 2026
A standalone original scene, not a client commission.

EXT. MUNICIPAL BEACH - EVENING

The sun hangs an inch above the Pacific. It does not move.

A folding table blocks the path to the water. Behind it, MARA, 34,
wears a city windbreaker and stamps forms with the weary precision
of someone trying to end a shift.

BEN, 60s, approaches carrying two paper cups and a small urn.

                         BEN
             Is it broken?

                         MARA
             Delayed. The city didn't renew the
             sunset permit.

She points to a notice taped to the table: NO DISPLAYS AFTER 6 P.M.

                         BEN
             My wife picked this beach.

Mara looks at the urn. Her stamp hovers over a rejection form.

                         MARA
             I'm sorry. Memorial dispersal is
             Tuesdays. Today is Thursday.

                         BEN
             She died on a Tuesday. We were busy.

He puts one coffee beside the urn. Mara checks the empty beach,
then a security camera aimed at her desk.

                         MARA
             If I let one person have a sunset,
             everyone will want one.

                         BEN
             That's generally how they work.

The sun gives a tiny, impatient shudder. Mara pulls a parking ticket
from beneath the table and stamps it APPROVED.

                         MARA
             This authorizes fifteen minutes.
             It doesn't authorize the urn.

                         BEN
             She always hated paperwork.

The sun finally slips into the water. For a moment, the beach turns
gold. Ben lifts the second cup toward the urn.

Mara's radio crackles.

                    SUPERVISOR (V.O.)
             Why is the sky orange?

Mara studies the ticket in her hand.

                         MARA
             Meter malfunction. I'll waive the fee.

She sets her own coffee beside Ben's, and they watch the last light
go out.
